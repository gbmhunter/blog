Wafer-Level Chip-Scale Package (WLCSP) is a very small SMD component package typically used for housing ICs. The WLCSP package can either be rectangular or square in shape.

The pin numbering follows the standard [BGA](/pcb-design/component-packages/bga-component-package/) convention: the rows are labelled with letters (A, B, C, ...) and the columns with numbers (1, 2, 3, ...). Thus pins are referred to as A1, C3, D11, etc.

The rows of bumps may be aligned in a grid or offset, again like BGA.

WLCSP is a true chip-scale packaging technology and offers the smallest possible footprint of any component package for a particular die.[^onsemi-an-5075-wlcsp]

## Synonyms

- LCSPW: W has changed position.

## Dimensions

The pin count is typically between 4 and 144 (2 × 2 to 12 × 12 bump arrays), and the pitch is typically between 0.3 and 0.5mm.[^onsemi-an-5075-wlcsp]

## Photosensitivity

Unusually, **the WLCSP package is sensitive to high-intensity, long wavelength light**. Because there is no opaque mould compound around the die, light hitting the exposed silicon generates electron-hole pairs in the die (the same effect a photodiode relies on). The resulting photocurrent can disrupt proper operation of the IC.

WLCSP packages can come with a backside laminate (BSL) which protects the die from chipping during handling and from light penetrating the back side.[^onsemi-an-5075-wlcsp] Not all WLCSP products feature a BSL. Fairchild Semiconductor (now part of onsemi) explain that having a BSL is not significant for board-level reliability.

<Image src={import('./_assets/paragraph-on-backside-laminate-for-wlcsp-package-farchild-an-5075.png')} width="517px">Paragraph on backside laminate (BSL) for WLCSP component packages. Image from an earlier Fairchild Semiconductor revision of AN-5075.[^onsemi-an-5075-wlcsp]</Image>

I encountered this issue first hand with a combined battery charger/linear regulator IC whose regulated output would collapse if I shone a strong 500W halogen at the PCB from about 1m away. Placing a bit of blue-tack over the IC fixed the issue.

## Raspberry Pi 2 Bug

The photosensitivity of the WLCSP package manifested itself as a bug on the Raspberry Pi 2. The regulator that provided power to the Raspberry Pi 2 core was in a WLCSP package, and when exposed to a high-intensity, long wavelength light (like a xenon camera flash), the Raspberry Pi 2 would reset.

The current caused by "normal" camera flashes does not seem to be able to permanently damage the chip.
