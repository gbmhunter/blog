The IMU can either consist of 3 separate chips mounted onto a small piece of PCB, or a single system-on-chip that integrated all of the components into one package.

They normally combine [accelerometers](/electronics/components/sensors/accelerometers/), gyroscopes and sometimes magnetometers together as a single device (monolithic package). Devices with accelerometers and gyroscopes are usually called 6-axis devices, while ones that also have magnetometers are called 9-axis devices.

They can include digital motion processors (DMPs). This offloads some of the processing required by the host microcontroller. They can also provide additional features such as motion gesture recognition. They do not normally include more advanced processing tasks such as an **attitude and heading reference system** (AHRS). This normally has to be provided by the host microcontroller.

The gyroscopes usually exploit the Coriolis effect and capacitive sensing to measure the angular rate about an axis.

Some IMUs negate the temperature drift by measuring their own temperature and performing self-compensation for these errors.

## Performance

The performance of IMU is normally expressed as a full-scale range and resolution for each sensor. The resolution might be specified in terms of the number of bits of the ADC. A "_Sensitivity Scale Factor_" might also be provided, which is the full-scale range divided by the resolution. Sensitivity is usually specified per least significant bit (LSB).

## Navigation

An IMU does not measure position directly. It outputs angular rates and accelerations, which the host integrates to estimate its current position from its last known position. This is called **dead reckoning**, and the errors accumulate over time. GPS does not have this problem.

## Communication

SPI is preferable over I2C when high data throughputs are required.

## GPS

More and more GPS units are beginning to support integration with an IMU unit to improve their accuracy.

## Software

[RTIMULib](https://github.com/RTIMULib/RTIMULib) is a pretty nice software package which makes it easier to get IMUs working with operating systems (including "embedded" ones such as the RaspberryPi). The writer, "richards-tech", also has a version of this for the Arduino, which makes it easy to use the Arduino as an I2C host to read back data from popular IMUs, send it via UART to a computer, where it can be processed.

## Packages

A big advantage of an IMU is the reduced PCB footprint due to the combination of multiple sensors into a single package. These are sometimes called system-in-packages (SIPs). Packages as small as 3 × 3 × 1 mm are common (e.g. the MPU-9250 below).

### ST ASM330LHB

The STMicroelectronics ASM330LHB is a 6-axis IMU designed for ASIL-B automotive applications. It comes in a [LGA-14 package](/pcb-design/component-packages/lga-component-package/) and contains a 3-axis accelerometer and 3-axis gyroscope.[^st-asm330lhb-imu-ds]

It supports the [I2C](/electronics/communication-protocols/i2c-communication-protocol/), [MIPI I3C](/electronics/communication-protocols/i3c-communication-protocol/) and [SPI](/electronics/communication-protocols/spi-communication-protocol/) communication interfaces for communication to a MCU.[^st-asm330lhb-imu-ds]

<Image src={import('./_assets/st-asm330lhb-lga-14l-package-photo.webp')} width="300px">The ST ASM330LHB 6-axis IMU in a LGA-14 package.[^st-asm330lhb-imu-ds]</Image>

One differentiating feature of the ASM330LHB is that it has been designed to meet ASIL-B automotive safety requirements. Two ASM330LHBs (for redundancy) can be used together with ST's "Safety Engine" firmware module to achieve ASIL-B.[^st-asm330lhb-asil-b-safety-manual] The image below shows the high-level architecture proposed by ST:

### Segway Balance Sensor Assembly

The Segway PT (released in 2001) uses a "Balance Sensor Assembly" designed by Silicon Sensing, built from multiple single-axis VSG3-based MEMS gyroscopes. Segway required the PT to keep balancing even if a component failed. To achieve this, the design has triple redundancy in the pitch axis and double redundancy in the roll and yaw axes.[^segway-nz-silicon-sensing-gyroscope] This is why the sensor boards are mounted at a number of different angles, rather than just three boards at right angles to one another. When this was designed, I suspect integrated 3-axis MEMS gyroscopes were not readily available, so multiple discrete single-axis sensors were needed.

<Image src={import('./_assets/redundant-imu-sensor-block-19457-00003-photo.webp')} width="800px">A "Balance Sensor Assembly" from a Segway PT, containing multiple single-axis Silicon Sensing VSG3-based MEMS gyroscopes mounted at different angles in a yellow plastic frame.[^segway-nz-silicon-sensing-gyroscope]</Image>

### Others

Analog Devices makes high-end military grade IMUs.

ST makes the iNEMO family of IMU modules.

DigiKey lists IMUs under the section [Sensors, Transducers -> Multifunction](http://www.digikey.com/product-search/en/sensors-transducers/multifunction/). The [STMicroelectronics LSM330TR](http://www.digikey.com/product-detail/en/LSM330TR/497-14381-1-ND/) is the cheapest accelerometer/gyro I could find on DigiKey as of Dec 2014 with a price of US\$3.40 (100).

ST and InvenSense are the dominant manufacturers of the chips used on the [Sparkfun IMU modules](https://www.sparkfun.com/categories/160).

The cheapest 9-axis IMU I have found on the internet ANYWHERE is the [GY-85 sold by DealExtreme](http://www.dx.com/p/gy-85-6dof-9dof-imu-sensor-module-for-arduino-148436), which as of Dec 2014 is US\$9.49 (1).
